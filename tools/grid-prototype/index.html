Floor-plan editor · v2

Rebuilt from the research. Real 360 dp phone width, real Sage & Gold tokens. A cell here is 34 dp — well under the 48 dp a fingertip reliably hits, which drives nearly every decision below.

Try it

  • Tap a room type, then press and hold on the plan. A dashed preview follows your finger and the chip at the top names the Vastu zone it will land in. Slide to adjust, lift to place.
  • The new room arrives already selected — you never have to discover "tap to select".
  • Touch a room and keep moving — it selects and moves in one gesture. Drag a corner to resize. Overlaps go red and are refused.
  • Every gesture has a button twin: ◀ ▲ ▼ ▶ to nudge, − + to size.

What the research changed from v1

  • Placement commits on lift, not on touch. A controlled experiment (Potter, Weldon & Shneiderman, CHI '88) found commit-on-lift with live feedback cut wrong-target errors by ~65% against commit-on-touch. Slightly slower, far more accurate — the right trade when placement decides the score.
  • Touch-down selects and the same finger keeps going into a move. Making people tap, lift, then drag doubles the gesture count and novices routinely never find step two.
  • The size chip is pinned to a fixed spot at the top rather than floating near the shape — a fixed position is never under the hand and needs no flip logic.
  • Directions, not coordinates. NORTH/EAST/SOUTH/WEST around the grid, the three Vastu bands drawn stronger, and the chip says "North-East" — the words the report will use.
  • A one-cell room gets a single corner grip, not four. Four 48 dp targets on a 34 dp square would bury the room and each other.
  • Neighbouring rooms are not dimmed. On a floor plan they're the reference you're aligning against.
  • Buttons for everything. WCAG 2.2 requires a non-drag path for every drag, and the most common complaint about every competitor is being forced to fight controls instead of entering a number.

Prototype for agreeing the interaction — not the shipping build.
