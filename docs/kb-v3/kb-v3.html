Read this firstWhat changed, and why this draft exists

Draft 2.0 was written from our intentions. This draft was written from the code. Every claim below was checked against the shipped rule files, and then a second reader was asked to prove each one wrong. What survived is here.

The document was behind

Your expert was shown 14 problem types. The app applies 30.

Draft 2.0's Section 10 listed fourteen. The shipped rule set holds thirty. Sixteen have never been in front of a reviewer — among them a kitchen in the south-west, a bedroom in the centre, a study in the south, a store in the north-east, a home that is long and narrow, and a general catch-all that fires whenever a room sits in a zone its own rule forbids.

More than half of what a paying customer can be told about their home has never been checked by anyone outside this project. All sixteen are set out below and are the most valuable part of this review.

A rule that was wrong

The engine was marking people down for the most auspicious shape in Vastu.

Only a south-west extension had a rule of its own. Every other part of a plan that stuck out — north-east included — fell through to the catch-all and was scored as a fault, under a heading telling the reader it sat somewhere its rule prohibits.

The contradiction was printed on the same page: the app's own text about a missing north-east corner reads "The same tradition welcomes a North-East extension", one paragraph above a number that had just docked them for exactly that. Fixed in this release. North-east, north and east extensions now raise nothing. South-west keeps its serious fault; south-east, north-west, south and west keep the catch-all. Cuts are untouched.

The most consequential thing in this review

A front door in the middle of your north wall scores 15 out of 100.

The app resolves a door to one of the thirty-two named positions by its bearing from the centre of the plot. The table is anchored so that the north side's eight positions run from 348.75° to 78.75° — rotated a third of a side clockwise from the north wall itself.

This is not an accident and not drift. Our own engine specification asks for exactly this anchoring, and the rule file implements it to the degree. But nobody has ever written down what it does to a real home, so here it is: a door dead in the centre of a north wall resolves to Naga, worth 15 out of 100, where the wall's own geometry would give Soma, worth 100. Seven of the eight compass points land on an unfavourable position. The bundled sample home's door sits on its south wall and is filed under an east-side position.

The front door is the highest-weighted single element in the entire reading. Draft 2.0 asked where the count begins and received no answer; this is what hangs on that answer. If our anchoring is right, most doors really are badly placed and the app is correct to say so. If it is out by a third of a side, then the heaviest number in every report we sell is wrong. We cannot tell which from the texts we have read. Please settle this one before anything else.

Answered since Draft 2.0

The prayer room is settled. The document was still asking.

Draft 2.0 asked whether the app should teach the classical centre or the modern north-east. That was ruled on 1 August 2026 and the app already follows it: north-east is ideal, north and east are acceptable, and no position is a fault — the classical centre included. A prayer room in the north-west reads as "not ideal", never as a defect, and the classical reading is still shown to the reader rather than being deleted.

Rules that cannot fire

Four rules exist in the rule set and can never reach a customer.

A mirror facing the bed and a beam over a bed or desk; a store or toilet tucked under the stairs; and a heavy fixture standing on the centre. Nothing in the app lets anyone record any of these, so the rules can never run. They make the rule set look larger than it is. They should either be given a question to answer or be struck out — please say which.

A promise the app does not keep

There is no school picker.

Draft 2.0's Section 13 says the app "shows both readings and lets the user pick a school profile". There is no such setting — it was removed because it read as jargon to a customer. The app shows both readings and scores by one of them. A leftover line in the engine still reads "other schools are coming soon"; it can never appear, and it is being removed.

Needs a Vastu ruling

A room labelled "BATH" escapes every check a room labelled "TOILET" receives.

The app treats TOILET, WC, WASHROOM, COMMON TOILET as a toilet — and a toilet in the north-east is the gravest finding it has. It treats BATH, BATHROOM, BATH ROOM and even BATH TOILET as a bathroom, and bathrooms carry no rule at all. They score nothing and print "Not rated".

Indian plans print "BATH" constantly. Two identical rooms therefore get either the app's gravest warning or complete silence, decided only by the draughtsman's choice of word. Whether a washing room should be judged like a waste room is a Vastu question, not a coding one. Please rule on it — see the open questions.

A decision worth re-confirming

Buyers and occupants are given no layout advice at all.

The report strips every "move it" suggestion for anyone who did not answer "I am building". That was a deliberate ruling in an earlier release, and it is defensible — a buyer cannot move a wall. But the app's first screen promises "exactly what to do about it" to all three audiences, so two of them are promised something the report then withholds.

Two corrections to the entries you did see

Draft 2.0 was wrong

X-06 does not flag the Pitra pada.

Draft 2.0 said the app flags an entrance "on an inauspicious pada, particularly the SW corner (Pitra)". It does not. The app flags a single arc from 213.75° to 236.25°, which contains Yama and Gandharva on the south side. Pitra sits at 258.75°, well outside it. The rule the document names as near-universal is not the rule the app runs. Please tell us which arc you would use.

Confirmed removed

"South-facing house" really is gone.

Draft 2.0 said it had been struck out. Confirmed: no such rule exists, the plan carries no facing field, and a test now fails the build if anyone reintroduces one.

Section 13, correctedWhere the schools disagree

The app carries ten disagreements, not the twelve Draft 2.0 listed. The cash locker is commercial and out of scope; the extent of the Brahmasthan has been settled at the central three by three of the nine by nine grid, so it is no longer shown as open.

A gap worth closing

Only one of the ten tells the reader which side we scored by.

The prayer-room entry ends with a plain sentence: "Your score follows the modern reading… The other school's advice never becomes a fault." The other nine show two readings and stop. A reader is left holding a disagreement with no idea which half moved their number.

We propose adding that sentence to all ten. Please tell us if any of them should be scored the other way round.

Cannot reach the reader

Five of the ten can never appear on screen.

A disagreement is shown only when the home actually contains the thing it is about. Five of them concern things the app never asks for — among them which way the bed faces, which way the idols face, and where the septic tank sits. They are in the rule set and no customer will ever see them.

Open questionsWhat we still cannot decide ourselves

Draft 2.0 asked eighteen. Three are answered. These are what remain, plus what this audit added. Each is a guess sitting inside a product we are selling.
