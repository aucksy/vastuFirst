Vastu Knowledge Base

The complete rule set behind a commercial Vastu application

This is every rule the application applies to a home, written out in full, with the provenance of each one stated honestly. We are asking two or three consultants from different schools to mark it — deliberately, so that disagreement between you surfaces rather than hides. Where we have guessed, we say so. Where the schools genuinely conflict, we show the reader both readings and score neither as a fault.

Product
An app for people about to buy or build a home. They supply a floor plan, mark which way north is, and are told what the tradition says about it.
Scope of this review
9 directions, the mandala, 32 door positions, 15 room rules, 30 faults, 28 remedies, 10 recorded disagreements.
Out of scope
The numerical weights and the 0–100 score. Those are a product invention with no basis in tradition. They are not yours to endorse and we will not claim you did.
What we need most
Strike anything that should not be there. A false alarm costs us more than a missed fault.

Before you beginHow to read this, and what we are asking

Every rule below carries a provenance tag. It is the most important column on the page, because it says how much authority we are claiming for that rule.

What would be most useful from you

Be blunt. A correction costs us nothing today. The same rule shipped to thousands of homes and found wrong later costs us the only thing this product is selling.

If you can point us to an actual verse for anything marked "derived", that rule becomes "textual" and we cite it in the app. That is the single most valuable thing you can give us.

We do not claim Vastu is scientifically established, and the app does not. It presents rules as traditional prescription, never as guaranteed outcome, and makes no health, wealth or fortune claim. That is both an honesty position and a legal one under Indian advertising and consumer law. Please do not soften your review on the assumption that we need the rules to promise results.

Please rule

The two we are least sure of.

The extent of the Brahmasthan. We use the central nine of eighty-one. Others teach the central four of sixty-four, or a ninth of the area however the plan divides. This decides whether a staircase is flagged or not on a great many plans.

A room spanning several padas. We use largest-share for good placements and any-encroachment for faults — deliberately asymmetric, so a room half in the north-east is warned about but not credited. Three methods are in use and we found no canonical rule. Is ours defensible, and should it differ between faults and good placements at all?

Section 3The main entrance — thirty-two positions

The highest-weighted single element in the entire reading. Each position spans 11.25°, eight to a side. The framework is traced to the Samarangana Sutradhara's dvaravinyasa.

Settle this before anything else

Where does the count begin?

We resolve a door by its bearing from the centre of the plot. Our table anchors the north side's eight positions from 348.75°, which is a third of a side clockwise of the north wall itself.

The effect is large and we would rather you heard it from us. A front door dead in the middle of a north wall resolves to Naga — one of the unfavourable positions — where an anchoring aligned to the wall would give Soma, one of the best. Under our anchoring, seven of the eight compass points land on an unfavourable position.

The front door is the heaviest thing in the reading. If our anchoring is right, then most doors genuinely are badly placed and the app is correct to say so. If it should be aligned to the walls, the most important number in every report is wrong. We cannot settle it from the texts we have read. Where does your count begin, and against what — the wall, or the compass?

Please rule

Four more room questions.

The prayer room rules nothing out, including the centre. We follow near-universal modern practice and place it in the north-east, with north and east acceptable. The classical position — Manasara reserves the centre for the family deity — is shown to the reader as the other reading but never scored as a fault. Is declining to condemn the centre the right call?

The centre is ruled out for six rooms and not the rest. A kitchen, a bedroom, a toilet or a living room in the centre is a fault; a study, a store, a dining room or a garage there is not. Should the open centre apply to every room without exception?

Utility rooms, corridors and courtyards are not scored. We have no rule for them. Should we?

There is no classical verse about toilets at all — the texts predate indoor sanitation. Every toilet rule we have is extrapolated from drainage and waste principles. Is that the right extrapolation?

We ship no crystals, no wind chimes, no yantras and no directional colours. We judged the first three to be modern commercial practice with visible Feng Shui influence, and the colour system to be a modern systematisation. Rivals sell all of them. Are we wrong to leave them out?

Section 8Flats, and what we can honestly offer

Most of our market buys a flat, not a plot — yet almost every Vastu rule is written for an independent house on land.

Please rule

Three questions about flats.

Which door governs? We apply the 32-position analysis to the flat's own door, the one from the common lobby, rather than the building gate. Is that the consensus?

Does the Brahmasthan mean anything in a flat? We apply it to the flat's own footprint. But a flat has no courtyard, and its centre is often a passage or a wall.

The honest question. A flat owner cannot move the building gate, the lift, the shafts or a shared wall. Does Vastu have anything meaningful to offer them beyond "choose a different flat"? An honest no is more useful to us than a marketable yes.

Section 9What we do not check, and say so

Stated here so you can tell us which of these matter enough to build.

Not checkedShould it be?
Ground slope and drainage. The principle that ground falls toward the North-East and water exits at the NE, N or E is one of the most consistent rules across every school we examined — and we do not ask about it at all.
Soil and site examination. The classical texts cover it at length; modern practice has largely dropped it. Should a pre-construction product revive it?
Plot shape as a named type — Gaumukhi, Shermukhi. We judge proportion but not the cow-faced / lion-faced distinction.
Compound wall heights, gate position, number of stair steps, kitchen platform direction, the direction a bed's head points. Each is offered as advice, none is scored, because we never ask for the input.
Commercial and industrial premises — cash counter, safe, owner's seat, machinery. Out of scope for this version entirely.
